The neutrino sector is the least understood part of the Standard Model of particle physics and may hold the key to the matter-antimatter asymmetry that explains our own existence. Although experiments conducted in 1998 and 2001 revealed that neutrinos have mass, the origin of that mass - which is millions of times smaller than the mass of the electron - remains unknown, as does its precise value. Cosmological measurements provide strict limits on neutrino mass, complementing particle physics experiments such as KATRIN. Furthermore, experiments like PTOLEMY aim to detect the cosmic neutrino background, providing information about the Universe just one second after the Big Bang.
