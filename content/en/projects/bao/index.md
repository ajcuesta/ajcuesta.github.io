These waves, which propagated when the Universe was in a plasma state, became frozen long before galaxies formed, thereby imprinting their signature onto the Universe's large-scale structure. They serve as excellent cosmic distance indicators, enabling the measurement of distances spanning billions of light-years with a precision of better than 1%. Indeed, current experiments such as DESI, Euclid, and Roman are conducting astronomical surveys that reach billions of years back in time, allowing for the reconstruction of the Universe's expansion history (thereby testing the validity of the standard ΛCDM cosmological model) and the formation of structures within it (thereby testing the validity of General Relativity).
