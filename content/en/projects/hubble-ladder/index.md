Measuring distances is a major challenge in cosmology. To do so, we need a thorough understanding of the properties of various astrophysical objects observable at great distances - such as Cepheid variable stars or Type Ia supernovae - and must precisely calibrate geometric distance measurements, such as stellar parallaxes or masers in nearby galaxies. In the near future, measuring quasars via strong gravitational lensing - enabled by the LSST survey at the Vera C. Rubin Observatory - and detecting gravitational waves from neutron star and/or black hole mergers will allow for a better quantification of the Hubble Tension, thereby advancing our understanding of the properties of mysterious dark energy.
